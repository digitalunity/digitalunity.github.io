How we help

Understand it. Design it. Make it real.

Each stage creates value in its own right. You can stop after diagnosis, take the design to another team, ask us to deliver it, or keep us involved only where our context continues to matter.

01

Operational Diagnosis

Clarity before commitment.

Bring us the part of the business that feels messy, repetitive, unreliable or difficult to change. We follow the real work across people, spreadsheets, systems and documents to establish what is actually happening and what is worth changing.

  • Follow representative real work rather than relying only on process descriptions.
  • Identify duplicated effort, waiting, hidden rules, workarounds and key-person dependency.
  • Make important states, ownership, hand-offs and sources of truth explicit.
  • Separate symptoms from causes and routine choreography from human judgement.
  • Leave you with a practical recommendation and sensible next options.

The answer does not have to be new software. A diagnosis is successful if it helps you make a better decision.

02

Solution Design & Architecture

Make the important decisions before the build.

Once the problem is understood, we design the improved way of working before implementation makes those decisions expensive to change.

  • Simplify unnecessary steps before automating them.
  • Define workflow, responsibility, business rules and data ownership.
  • Decide what should remain human and what can become deterministic.
  • Compare process change, existing products, integration, automation, AI and bespoke engineering honestly.
  • Design integration failure behaviour, security, migration, reporting, adoption and implementation sequencing where relevant.

The design should be usable by a competent internal or external delivery team. It is not written to lock you into Digital Unity.

03

Delivery & Stewardship

Deliver the outcome without creating accidental dependency.

We can take the design through to reality ourselves, work alongside your developers, lead the difficult integration or migration work, or act as technical authority while another team implements.

  • Deliver in useful slices and test assumptions against real users and data.
  • Keep the implementation aligned with the operational outcome, not just the ticket list.
  • Make acceptance, handover, support and ownership explicit.
  • Stay involved as a steward only where continued architectural or operational attention creates clear value.
  • Simplify, hand over or retire technology when that is healthier than preserving it.

A healthy engagement can end with Digital Unity doing less hands-on work because the client is stronger and ownership has moved to the right place.
