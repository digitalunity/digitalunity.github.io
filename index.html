The starting point

Technology follows the problem.

We start with how the work actually happens. We follow the information, hand-offs, decisions and exceptions, make the important rules and responsibilities explicit, then work out what is genuinely worth changing.

The answer might be process change, better use of existing software, integration, automation, AI, bespoke engineering — or less technology, not more.

What this often looks like

Too much chasing

People spend their time checking what has arrived, what is missing and who needs to act next.

Spreadsheets become the real system

The formal software contains the data, but the team trusts a tracker, inbox or workaround to understand what is actually happening.

Old systems become hard to change

Important business knowledge is buried in years of behaviour, integrations and exceptions that nobody wants to break.

The same information is entered twice

Customers, staff or finance teams repeat work because systems do not share responsibility or data cleanly.

Ownership is unclear

Work sits between people until somebody remembers, chases or reconstructs what should happen next.

Reports do not match reality

The numbers exist, but the underlying state or meaning is not captured consistently enough to trust the answer.
