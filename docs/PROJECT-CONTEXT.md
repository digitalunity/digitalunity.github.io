# Digital Unity Website — Project Context

## Why this document exists

This document gives developers and agents enough context to work safely on the Digital Unity public website without reconstructing the business proposition from scratch.

It is a repository-local summary of the current Digital Unity market proposition and website decisions. It does **not** replace the full internal Digital Unity strategy/evidence vault.

## What Digital Unity is

Digital Unity is an operational-systems consultancy and engineering practice.

The working public proposition is:

> **Digital Unity helps businesses untangle messy operations across people, spreadsheets and systems so they become easier to understand, easier to run and easier to improve.**

The core differentiation is:

> **Technology follows the problem.**

Digital Unity starts by understanding how work actually moves through the business, making business meaning, responsibility and information flow explicit, then choosing the smallest appropriate combination of process change and technology.

The technology may include existing products, configuration, integration, automation, AI or bespoke software. No particular technology is assumed in advance.

## Buyer problems

The strongest buyer-recognisable problem language identified during proposition work includes:

- repeated data entry;
- spreadsheets becoming unofficial operational systems;
- work getting stuck between people;
- constant chasing;
- business rules living in people's heads;
- disconnected systems;
- software that technically works while the operation around it does not;
- reporting that does not reflect operational reality;
- mature or legacy systems becoming difficult to change;
- technology being purchased while the manual administration remains.

Useful emotional language includes:

- firefighting;
- not trusting the information;
- being held together with string;
- being frightened to change an old system;
- having outgrown the current way of working.

The desired end-state is not "more software". It is an operation that feels under control again.

## Desired outcome

The current enduring outcome phrase is:

> **Easier to understand. Easier to run. Easier to improve.**

Supporting outcome language includes:

- less chasing;
- less re-keying;
- clearer ownership;
- better visibility;
- fewer workarounds;
- routine coordination happening automatically where appropriate;
- people spending more attention on decisions that genuinely require judgement.

## Service model

### 1. Operational Diagnosis

**Understand it.**

Purpose:

> Work out what is really happening, what is getting in the way and what is worth changing.

Digital Unity follows representative real work across people, systems, spreadsheets, documents and communications. It identifies states, responsibilities, hand-offs, business rules, workarounds, sources of truth, repetitive choreography and human judgement points.

A successful diagnosis may recommend process change, better use of an existing system, integration, automation, design work, or no technology project at all.

Commercial thought:

> **Clarity before commitment.**

### 2. Solution Design & Architecture

**Design it.**

Purpose:

> Turn an understood operational problem into an implementable design for the improved operation.

The design may cover target workflow, responsibilities, states, business rules, data ownership, integration boundaries, human/automation split, build-vs-buy decisions, security, reporting, migration, adoption and implementation sequencing.

A key principle is to simplify before automating.

Commercial thought:

> **Make the important decisions before the build.**

### 3. Delivery & Stewardship

**Make it real.**

Digital Unity may deliver directly, work alongside an internal team, lead difficult integration/migration boundaries, or act as technical authority while another supplier implements.

The implementation must remain tied to the operational outcome rather than merely closing technical tickets.

Long-term stewardship is optional and should have an explicit purpose. Handover, simplification and decommissioning are valid success states.

Commercial thought:

> **Deliver the outcome without creating accidental dependency.**

## Evidence base

The current website uses six flagship case studies.

### Custom Kitchens

Long-running manufacturing system work requiring Digital Unity to understand cabinet/manufacturing rules, factory state, machine outputs, logistics and delivery.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity turned hidden manufacturing rules into an operational system that could evolve with the factory.

Strong themes:

- domain reconstruction;
- manufacturing workflow;
- tacit rules;
- physical/digital hand-offs;
- long-term stewardship.

### Abbey Taylor Jones

Operational CRM for insolvency research/calling/case workflow, followed years later by live-estate renewal and simplification.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity built the operational CRM, then returned later to untangle complexity accumulated through real use and make the live operation explicit again.

Strong themes:

- operational CRM;
- source-of-truth reasoning;
- adoption;
- explicit state;
- legacy renewal;
- responsible decoupling.

### iProsurv

Drone-inspection workflow spanning office staff, pilots, customers, safety evidence, media processing, human audit and archive/restore.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity turned a drone-inspection service into an explicit operational lifecycle from assignment and safety evidence through media audit, client release and archive.

Strong themes:

- role/state modelling;
- safety-sensitive workflow;
- field operations;
- human judgement;
- large-media architecture.

### Whinfrey Briggs

Contractor payroll administration involving agency sheets, expenses, invoices, payslips, notifications and BACS preparation.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity automated repetitive payroll choreography without automating away the people accountable for the money.

Strong themes:

- practitioner-derived rules;
- financial workflow;
- selective automation;
- explicit approval;
- later simplification/retirement.

### BE-Together

Multi-party education/placement/training operation involving schools, students, employers, tutors, host families, auditors, evidence and finance.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity created an operational system where multiple parties could contribute the right information and the business could see what needed to happen next.

Strong themes:

- multi-party coordination;
- state/responsibility;
- controlled self-service;
- evidence/document workflow;
- reminders and routine follow-up.

### Food Test Labs

Staged modernisation of a laboratory/customer-system estate and escape from a fragile third-party portal dependency.

Key proof:

> Digital Unity helped the laboratory escape a fragile dependency, modernised the platform in stages and deliberately transferred increasing capability to the client's own developer.

Strong themes:

- stabilise before modernise;
- legacy reconstruction;
- proportionate architecture;
- security/infrastructure;
- capability transfer;
- technical authority.

## Cross-case proof

The strongest recurring claims across the six cases are:

1. Digital Unity starts from the operation rather than a predetermined technology.
2. Important business meaning is often tacit and must be recovered from people and real work.
3. Workflow, state, responsibility and next action are central design concepts.
4. Routine coordination can often be automated while accountable judgement remains human.
5. Technology choices should follow operational constraints.
6. Exceptions and failure paths matter as much as happy paths.
7. Integrations are real boundaries with differing meanings and failure modes.
8. Live use continues to reveal rules and assumptions after launch.
9. Long-term involvement should evolve as the client's capability and needs change.

Secondary but meaningful themes include adoption, security, historical evidence, simplification/decommissioning and client capability transfer.

## Evidence caveat

The historical archive strongly demonstrates the method and nature of the work, but formal quantitative before/after outcome measurement was inconsistent.

Do not invent claims such as:

- 70% admin reduction;
- 2x productivity;
- guaranteed ROI;
- fixed percentage error reduction;
- hundreds of hours saved;

unless new primary evidence is added.

Future engagements should deliberately capture relevant baselines, adoption evidence and after-state measures.

## Commercial hypotheses

The following are internal market-test anchors, not necessarily public website prices:

- Focused Operational Diagnosis: around **£3,250**
- Typical multi-system diagnosis: around **£6,000**
- Bounded Solution Design: around **£4,750**
- Typical multi-system Solution Design & Architecture: around **£8,500**
- Delivery: scoped from the agreed design; internal senior day-equivalent sanity check around **£900–£1,100**
- Stewardship: approximately **£500 to £5,000+ per month** depending on responsibility

Important commercial principles:

- paid thinking;
- each stage stands alone;
- responsibility is priced;
- scope change is explicit;
- support is not implied;
- concessions should be visible;
- client-funded work and Digital Unity product investment remain separate.

Do not publish these figures automatically. The current website is testing the proposition before a final public pricing decision.

## Tone and design direction

The current visual direction is deliberately restrained, calm and professional.

The site should feel like a senior technical/operational practice rather than a fashionable agency landing page.

Avoid by default:

- excessive animation;
- loud gradients;
- generic stock-office imagery;
- AI imagery;
- giant technology-logo walls;
- trend-led design that overwhelms the proposition.

Content should lead with business symptoms and outcomes before technical implementation detail.
