Selected work

Different industries. The same need to understand how the work really happens.

These cases are deliberately varied. They show Digital Unity working across physical manufacturing, finance, field operations, multi-party services and mature legacy estates without starting from a fixed technology product.

Not just delivery moments

Some of the strongest evidence comes from what happened years later.

Several relationships have involved returning to systems after real use changed the business around them. That long view matters: it exposes which rules survived, which assumptions aged badly, where ownership weakened and what should be simplified rather than preserved.

Manufacturing · Custom Kitchens

Turning hidden factory rules into an operational system that could evolve with the factory.

Digital Unity learned the real order and manufacturing process, preserved useful behaviour from the incumbent system, represented cabinet and production rules explicitly, and connected office order entry with factory production, machine outputs, logistics and delivery.

The relationship later returned to machinery changes, delivery, trade ordering and operational visibility — evidence that the useful work was not a one-off build, but keeping the system aligned with how the factory itself evolved.

Insolvency operations · Abbey Taylor Jones

Building the operational CRM, then returning years later to untangle what real use had accumulated.

The original CRM coordinated imports, research, calling, callbacks, correspondence and later case hand-offs. Recent work has re-examined the live estate where workflow meaning became spread across flags, call reasons, portal behaviour and parallel spreadsheets.

The return engagement is part of the proof: mature systems accumulate operational history, adoption patterns and side effects that cannot be understood safely from code alone.

Drone inspections · iProsurv

Making responsibility, evidence and state explicit from field assignment through audited client delivery.

Digital Unity modelled the inspection lifecycle across office teams, pilots and customers, including safety gates, completion evidence, human audit, large-media transfer and later archive and restore.

Evidence discipline

We would rather make a smaller claim we can support than invent a bigger result.

The historical evidence is strongest on operational reasoning, workflow, architecture, system longevity and how the work evolved. It is less consistent on formal before-and-after ROI or productivity measurement. We are deliberately correcting that by capturing relevant baselines and outcome evidence on future engagements.
